    About this map

    The shape of the whole ocean floor from GEBCO 2026, and how little of it ships have actually measured. Key numbers, FAQ and how to cite.

    Data sources and licences
    Privacy

    No cookies, no analytics, no account. Panel and layer preferences stay in your browser’s local storage.

    Land imagery comes from Esri (server.arcgisonline.com). Switch off Satellite land under Layers and nothing is requested from Esri.

    The site, including the zoom 6–7 depth tiles, is served by Cloudflare Pages, which logs IP addresses (a mirror on GitHub Pages reads those tiles from Hugging Face). Other hosts are contacted only when you use what needs them: Wikimedia Commons for a wreck photo when its card opens, and USGS for live earthquakes when you switch them on.

    Disclaimer

    Data are provided as is, without warranty of any kind. Depths derived from GEBCO are not for navigation.

    Each layer keeps its source licence (some are non-commercial); see LICENSES.md in the repository.

    Accessibility

    Every control works from the keyboard with a visible focus ring, panels are labelled for screen readers, and reduced-motion settings stop the animations.

      Seafloor

      • Land is shown from satellite and aerial photos; the ocean keeps the GEBCO 2026 relief colours (pale shelf to violet-black trenches). Switch it off for plain grey land, and nothing is loaded from Esri.

        Esri World Imagery. Powered by Esri · Source: Esri, Vantor, Earthstar Geographics, and the GIS User Community.

      • Loading coverage statistics…

        Dims every cell that ships have not surveyed directly; those depths are estimated from satellite gravity or interpolation.

        GEBCO 2026 type identifier grid, codes 10–17 = direct survey.

      • Seafloor newly counted as directly measured since the 2025 grid: new surveys, and older ones newly shared.

        GEBCO 2025 and 2026 type identifier grids, compared cell by cell.

      • Where tectonic plates meet, coloured by how they move. Click a boundary for the two plates and their speed.

        Bird (2003) PB2002, ODC-By 1.0.

      • Which earthquakes

        Every earthquake of magnitude 5.5 or more since 1900, sized by magnitude and coloured by depth.

        Early decades miss smaller events and depths are often fixed defaults; magnitudes are of mixed types. Click a circle for its card and the USGS event page.

      • 69,770 undersea mountains at least 100 m tall, from two satellite-gravity catalogues.

        Filled: a sonar sounding sits in the summit cell. Open ring: the summit depth is predicted from gravity. Taller seamounts first; the small ones appear as you zoom in.

      • Trenches, ridges, seamounts and basins, larger ones first. Click a name for its card.

        IHO-IOC GEBCO Gazetteer of Undersea Feature Names.

      • Depth certainty

      • A second global model (AUST_2026_BAT) minus GEBCO 2026, shown only deeper than 1 km and where most of the cell is unmeasured. Both lean on satellite gravity, so agreement does not prove a depth right: read it as a lower bound on the uncertainty.

        Yuan & Yang (2026), doi:10.5281/zenodo.22711109, CC BY 4.0.

      • How sure a machine-learning model using SWOT satellite gravity is of its own predicted depth, in three classes. It describes that model, not the GEBCO grid; cells with a ship sounding are left clear.

        Nilsson, Phrampus, Sandwell et al. (2025), doi:10.5281/zenodo.17673974, CC BY 4.0.

      • Southern Ocean seals carrying depth tags, compared with the chart.

        Coloured dots: places where seals dived more than 20 m deeper than GEBCO 2026 allows, beyond each dive’s position error. Faint dots: where seals dived at all. A dive can show the chart is too shallow, never too deep. Click a dot for its numbers.

        SO-ABBA v1.0 (Hindell, McMahon, Jonsen et al. 2026), doi:10.5281/zenodo.19121676, CC BY 4.0.

      Ocean

      • Temperature, salinity and density at 11 depths and at the seafloor, averaged over 1991–2020. Switching it on opens the Ocean water tool.

        World Ocean Atlas 2023, NOAA NCEI.

      • What to show

        A group shows where its OBIS records are, per 1.4° cell.

        Records show where people looked as much as where life is: coasts, rich countries and research cruises dominate. ES50 corrects for the number of records, not for where or what was sampled. AquaMaps is a model, smooth by design. Click the map for a cell’s most-recorded species.

        OBIS (2026) www.obis.org; OBIS speciesgrids (OBIS + GBIF), CC BY-NC 4.0; AquaMaps (version 10/2019), CC BY-NC 3.0.

      • Chlorophyll-a seen from space, a measure of floating algae: from 0.02 mg/m³ in the South Pacific gyre to over 3 in the Peru upwelling.

        Log scale, averaged over July 2002 to December 2025. Lakes are masked. Coastal values run high in muddy water, and polar seas have no data in winter.

        Chlorophyll-a: NASA OBPG, MODIS-Aqua R2022.0 climatology (doi:10.5067/AQUA/MODIS/L3M/CHL/2022.0)

      • Mean wind 10 m above the surface, 1991–2020: the trade winds, the westerlies and the monsoon that reverses over the Arabian Sea (from the NE in January, from the SW in July).

        A monthly mean is a vector average, so it is calmer than any single day where winds change direction. Point at the map for speed and direction.

        Winds: NCEP/NCAR Reanalysis 1, 1991-2020 monthly means (NOAA PSL)

      • Surface currents (top 30 m), 2015–2024: the Gulf Stream, Kuroshio and Agulhas reach about 1.3 m/s even as monthly means.

        Averaging smooths eddies and meanders, so the jets look broader and slower than on any one day; no data under sea ice. One flow layer at a time: winds or currents.

        Currents: OSCAR v2.0 (ESR, NASA PO.DAAC), 2015-2024 monthly means

      • Measured, not modelled: a line of moorings across the Atlantic at 26.5° N has recorded the overturning since April 2004, about 17 million m³ of water per second.

        Warm water flows north near the surface, sinks in the far north and returns south between 1,100 and 5,000 m; a little Antarctic water creeps north along the bottom. Click the line for the monthly record.

        RAPID-MOCHA-WBTS v2024-2 (NOC, University of Miami, NOAA AOML), UK Open Government Licence v3.0
